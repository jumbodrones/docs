Drone Platforms

Three drone platforms

Multi-Rotor
The most common platform — tri, quad, hex, and octo configurations with fixed pitched blades.
  • Unique range of movement
  • VTOL and hover flight
  • Good camera control
  • Operates in confined areas
Fixed-Wing
Aerodynamic, single-wing airframes built for distance and speed.
  • Longer duration
  • Excels at linear flights
  • Generally safer
  • Large area coverage
Hybrid VTOL
Less common — pairs propeller lift with a fixed wing, propeller and wing together.
  • Dynamic range of movement
  • Vertical takeoff and landing
  • Long-endurance flight
Walk through each card left to right. Multi-rotor is our workhorse for close-in, confined-space work. Fixed-wing trades hover for range and speed. Hybrid VTOL is the newest addition — best of both, but still maturing.
Weighing the tradeoffs

Pros, cons, and price by platform

Multi-Rotor
Pros
  • Accessibility & ease of use
  • VTOL and hover flight
  • Good camera control
  • Operates in confined areas
Cons
  • Short flight times
  • Small payload capacity
Uses: aerial photo & video, inspection, SAR
Price: $5K–$50K (sensors drive up costs)
Fixed-Wing
Pros
  • Long endurance
  • Large area coverage
  • Fast flight speed
Cons
  • Needs space to launch & land
  • No VTOL or hover, can't fly backwards
  • Harder to fly, more training
  • Expensive
Uses: aerial mapping, pipeline & power line inspection
Price: $25K–$40K (sensors drive up costs)
Hybrid VTOL
Pros
  • Vertical takeoff and landing
  • Long-endurance flight
Cons
  • Not perfect at hovering or forward flight
  • Still in development
Uses: large area mapping
Price: ~ $35K - $75K depending on sensors
Price ranges are driven mostly by sensor payload, not the airframe itself — call that out. Note multi-rotor's short flight time vs. fixed-wing's training/space requirements as the key tradeoffs mission planners weigh.
