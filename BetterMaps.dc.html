Mission Planning · Camera Shutter

Global vs. rolling shutter

Global shutter
Captures the entire image in one instant — giving an exact camera position for every photo, ideal for accurate 3D reconstruction.
Rolling shutter
Exposes the image in sections over time — no single exact camera position, which can warp objects in the scene and reduce accuracy.
Rolling shutter can visibly warp fast-moving subjects, like this propeller blade, as different rows expose at different times. Pix4D and other applications offer rolling-shutter corrections; alternatively, program the flight to stop and hover at each capture. Credit: Carlson Software.
Mission Planning · Ground Sample Distance (GSD)

Pixel center to pixel center
GSD
Each square is one pixel
GSD is the real-world ground distance between the centers of two adjacent pixels — not the pixel's size on screen, but what it represents on the ground.
i.e. — spatial resolution / pixel size
A smaller GSD means each pixel covers less ground, so the image shows finer detail.
Mission Planning · Ground Sample Distance (GSD)

The distance between two pixel centers

GSD is the real-world distance between two adjacent pixel centers — in other words, spatial resolution, or pixel size.

GSD = (Flight Height × Sensor Size) ÷ (Focal Length × Image Size)
Mission Planning · GSD & Tradeoffs

Smaller GSD vs. larger GSD

Smaller GSD
More detail
→ Lower flying height
→ Longer flight time
→ Larger datasets
→ Longer processing times
Larger GSD
Less detail
→ Higher flying altitude
→ Shorter flight time
→ Smaller datasets
→ Moderate processing times
GSD can give us some indication of accuracy.
